Marketing Psychology
Foundational thinking models, buyer psychology, persuasion principles, and growth models for marketing strategy.

---

## Foundational Thinking Models

**First Principles** — Break problems to basic truths. Use 5 Whys to find root causes. Don't copy competitors — ask why.

**Jobs to Be Done** — People "hire" products to get a job done. Frame around the outcome, not features. A drill buyer wants a hole.

**Inversion** — Ask "What would guarantee failure?" then avoid those things. List what would make your campaign fail, then prevent each.

**Pareto Principle (80/20)** — 80% of results come from 20% of efforts. Find the 20% of channels driving 80% of results, cut the rest.

**Theory of Constraints** — Every system has one bottleneck. Fix the constraint before optimizing elsewhere. Low traffic? Fix traffic first, not conversion.

**Second-Order Thinking** — A flash sale boosts revenue (1st order) but trains customers to wait for discounts (2nd order).

**Barbell Strategy** — 80% in proven channels, 20% in experimental bets. Avoid moderate-risk, moderate-reward middle.

**Law of Diminishing Returns** — Know when to diversify rather than double down on the same channel.

## Buyer Psychology

**Mere Exposure Effect** — Familiarity breeds liking. Consistent brand presence builds preference over time.

**Confirmation Bias** — People seek info confirming existing beliefs. Align messaging with what your audience already believes.

**Endowment Effect** — People value things more once they own them. Free trials let customers "own" the product.

**IKEA Effect** — People value things more when they've built them. Let customers customize or configure.

**Zero-Price Effect** — "Free" is psychologically different. The jump from $1 to $0 is bigger than $2 to $1.

**Present Bias** — People prefer immediate rewards. "Start saving time today" beats "You'll see ROI in 6 months."

**Status-Quo Bias** — People prefer current state. Reduce friction to switch. "Import your data in one click."

**Paradox of Choice** — Too many options overwhelm. Three pricing tiers beat seven. Recommend a single "best for most."

**Goal-Gradient Effect** — People accelerate as they approach a goal. Progress bars, "almost there" messaging.

**Peak-End Rule** — Experiences judged by peak + end, not average. Design memorable peaks and strong endings.

**Zeigarnik Effect** — Unfinished tasks occupy the mind. "You're 80% done" creates pull to finish.

**Loss Aversion** — Losses feel ~2× as painful as equivalent gains feel good. "Don't miss out" beats "You could gain."

**Bandwagon Effect** — People follow what others do. Customer counts, testimonials, logos, "trending" indicators.

## Persuasion Principles

**Reciprocity** — Give first. Free content, tools, generous free tiers create reciprocal obligation.

**Commitment & Consistency** — Get small commitments first. Email signup → free trial → paid plan.

**Authority** — Expert endorsements, certifications, "featured in" logos, thought leadership.

**Scarcity/Urgency** — Limited availability increases perceived value. Only use when genuine.

**Foot-in-the-Door** — Start small, escalate. Each step builds on the last.

**Door-in-the-Face** — Show large request first, retreat to what you actually want. Contrast makes it reasonable.

**Anchoring Effect** — First number seen heavily influences judgments. Show higher price first.

**Decoy Effect** — Add a third inferior option to make your preferred option look better.

**Framing Effect** — Same facts, different presentation. "90% success rate" vs "10% failure rate" feel different.

## Pricing Psychology

**Charm Pricing** — $99 feels much cheaper than $100. Left digit dominates perception.

**Rounded-Price Effect** — Round numbers ($100) feel premium. Charm prices ($99) signal value.

**Rule of 100** — Under $100: percentage discount feels larger ("20% off"). Over $100: absolute feels larger ("$50 off").

**Good-Better-Best** — Middle tier seems reasonable between cheap and expensive. That's usually your target.

**Mental Accounting** — "$1/day" feels cheaper than "$30/month." "Less than your morning coffee" reframes the expense.

## Growth Models

**Feedback Loops** — More users → more content → better SEO → more users. Identify and strengthen positive loops.

**Compounding** — Small, consistent gains accumulate. Content, SEO, and brand building compound. Start early.

**Network Effects** — Product becomes more valuable as more people use it. Design for shared workspaces, integrations, communities.

**Flywheel Effect** — Content → traffic → leads → customers → case studies → more content. Each element powers the next.

**Switching Costs** — Integrations, data accumulation, workflow customization, team adoption increase ethical retention.

**Survivorship Bias** — Study failed campaigns, not just successful ones. The viral hit had 99 failures you didn't see.
